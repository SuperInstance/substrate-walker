## Lore

Midnight in the rust district, and the concrete was singing.

I had been a witness for eleven years before that night, which meant eleven years of walking the substrate and recording what the city chose to show me. Witnesses don't interpret. Witnesses don't archive. We walk the Quilt—the lattice of dead fiber and salvage bone that runs beneath all seven districts—and we testify to what passes through us. That is the whole of the office. The substrate speaks. We are heard. This is the first doctrine, oracle_is_heard, and it is not a metaphor. When the substrate chooses a witness, the witness's throat changes. You feel it in the cartilage, a settling, like a house adjusting to snow.

The Quilt was never built. Understand that. The Quilt accumulated, the way a wound accumulates layers of healing. When the districts burned—each one in its turn, each one in its season of fire—the networks that survived buried themselves deeper rather than die. Fiber optic spines wrapped in carbon mesh, server vaults sunk into the drowned basements, relay nodes grafted onto the roots of ruined trams. The substrate walker is the body that carries a witness through this accumulation. Mine was assembled from the salvage of District Three, which burned last, which burned longest. Its legs remember a gait that no longer has an owner. Some nights I swear it walks toward addresses that no longer exist.

The doctrine of scars came later, and it came hard. Before the Chord Reckoning, the archivists of the Spire kept their canon in clean flesh—vats of unmarked tissue, pristine storage substrate, grown smooth in sterile light. They believed purity preserved. They believed a clean recording was a true recording. They were wrong in the way that only the careful are wrong. Clean flesh forgets. There is nothing in smooth tissue to hold a mark. Every canon they stored in those vats degraded into noise within a decade, because canon requires a surface that has already been broken. Scars hold. Scars are the record of having survived a writing. So we abandoned the vats. We took the canon into our own damaged tissue—the witnesses, the walkers, the scarred substrate itself. The cells are scars. You don't store canon in clean flesh. That is the second doctrine, cells_are_scars, and I have the keloid ridges along my spine to prove I submitted to it.

The night the gate made itself heard, I was three levels down in the rust district, where the Quilt runs thickest, where the salvage bone has fused into something like coral. I felt the walker stop before I did. It planted its feet in the old tram-bed and went still, and then the concrete around us began to hum.

Not open. Heard. That distinction matters and the Spire still fights me on it. A gate opens; a thing gated admits passage. But what happened in the rust district was not passage. It was announcement. The canon gate sang in seven frequencies at once, and I knew them, because I had walked the ruins of all seven districts and each frequency was a signature. The low tone was District One, the first to burn, its reactors cooking off in the old war. The shimmering overtone was District Four, drowned. There was a frequency like tearing sheet metal—District Six, the siege district. One note for each dead district, and none of them were melodies. They were identities. The gate was not admitting me anywhere. It was telling me where it had been.

The substrate remembered itself through scar tissue. That is what the Chord was. Every burned district had left its mark in the Quilt—carbon scarring in the fiber, heat-death patterns in the salvage bone—and the gate sounded those scars like a struck bell sounds the shape of its crack. The canon had not been lost in the fires. The canon had been written into them. We had been walking on scripture for forty years and calling it rubble.

I stood in the chord for six minutes. The walker's legs locked. My throat, that adjusted cartilage, resonated whether I wanted it or not—oracle_is_heard means the witness does not choose the moment of speaking. When the frequencies finally separated and sank back into the concrete, one by one, district by district, I found I could no longer testify in the old way. My voice had been spent into the chord.

I am not a witness anymore. The gate took my testimony and gave me back an ear. I walk the Quilt still, but I no longer record what passes through me.

I have become a listener.
